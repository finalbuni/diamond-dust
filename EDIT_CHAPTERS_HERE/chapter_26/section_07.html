Yeehyeon straightened his back and slowly turned toward the window.

Although evening was approaching, the sky outside remained as bright and blue as midday. Yet, whether it was his mood or simply the angle of the setting sun, a shadow seemed to have fallen over the room, regardless of the light outside.

Yeehyeon set down the bundle of papers in his hand and got off the bed. He walked to the small sink in the corner across from the bed, poured himself a glass of tap water, and drank it. Then, as if that wasn't enough, he began to wash his face.

Even splashing cold water over his face several times did little to restore his sense of reality. Everything before his eyes felt at once familiar and strange, like someone who had been held captive for a long time and had only just found their way home.

Without bothering to dry his face, he stumbled backward like a dazed person and leaned against the sink. The contents he had pulled from the box — which he had eagerly torn open the moment he returned to his room — lay scattered across the bed.

What Marcus had sent were a long, handwritten letter; a diary in the most worn condition Yeehyeon had ever seen; and a bundle of typed pages transcribing the diary's contents, though portions were smudged or molded to the point of illegibility.

Nearly half the pages still remained, but he couldn't bring himself to read any further.

Yeehyeon swallowed dryly and slowly dragged his hands over his wet face. As Ben had said, the room was stifling — yet goosebumps rose along his skin. The silence around him was unsettlingly deep. As if the room had been submerged underwater, the familiar background noise he usually took for granted was entirely blocked out.

As if grateful to have something to do with his hands, Yeehyeon walked over to the desk by the window and took out a cigarette and a lighter. The same brand Liu smoked. He had bought them sometime around the beginning of spring and the pack was about half full. They tasted stale and dry, but he wasn't smoking for enjoyment.

Being sure that Liu was keeping an eye on him did nothing to ease the ache of missing him.

He started his days early, worked in two sessions, one in the morning and one in the afternoon, studied English and French, and helped out with office chores when he had time. His colleagues at The Hands would tease him with a note of admiration, saying he lived less like a painter than a diligent office worker or civil servant, and that he was like a monk, untroubled by worldly cares. But the reality was different.

He was not okay. He was not at peace.

"When things are hard — mentally or physically — it's best to keep your usual routine as much as you can."

He was simply enduring the pain by holding his routine together, by not letting it crack, by refusing to give the shadowy things inside him any opening to crawl through.

The reason Nicholas couldn't tell his father he had manifested as an Omega. The reason the diary's owner couldn't bring himself to speak openly to Erich about the pheromones he sensed from him. The reason Liu couldn't reveal his true identity. Though the forms were slightly different, the underlying foundation of each concealment was essentially the same.

He understood now that his own passivity — not holding Alphas and Omegas in contempt, but failing to seek understanding or knowledge of them as fellow members of society — had itself been a form of Beta-centric thinking.

Yeehyeon leaned against the open window and exhaled smoke slowly, his breath carrying a faint tremor. Before he'd taken three full drags, tears slid down his cheeks. He didn't try to hold them back.

He'd held back for so long. He wanted to tell Liu how much he missed him, how much he still loved him. He wanted to leave his whole life here behind, go back to Seoul, and hold him. He wanted to feel Liu suck hard on his lower lip.

He'd wanted Liu inside him. He'd wanted to feel him knot, to see him moan with an Alpha's pleasure, and to be the one who made him feel it.

That fierce longing for Liu couldn't be explained by pheromones alone—not by Erich's, which only the person who kept the diary could sense, or by Yeehyeon's own, which Liu alone seemed to have sensed. The understanding and closeness that had grown between them over time couldn't have been the work of pheromones.

And yet, in bed with Liu, Yeehyeon had been so passionate and bold that he hardly recognized himself. That, too, couldn't be explained by love alone.

Leaning his head against the window frame, Yeehyeon let his hand drop, forgetting even the cigarette. He let himself think about Liu as much as he wanted, without holding back, shying away, or trying to make what he felt seem smaller.

If the Changing was Liu's greed, his mistake, his undeniable sin, then the silence he kept when confronted about it could only be understood as love. Liu could have tried to lighten the weight of what he'd done by telling Yeehyeon that he had pheromones of his own. He hadn't.

Liu had wanted Yeehyeon to be free to choose his own future without being bound by guilt or obligation. That love, too, hadn't come from pheromones.

When all Liu could say was “I love you,” Yeehyeon was glad he hadn't called Liu's love a lie. He was glad he hadn't denied that love altogether, when he'd felt it so clearly in the tremor of his skin and in the trembling deep inside him.

He went on sobbing quietly for a long time, like someone looking back from the top of a long climb and slowly catching his breath.

Though it was nearing nine o'clock, the sky over Paris was still a bright, unwavering blue.
