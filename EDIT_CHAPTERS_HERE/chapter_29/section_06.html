Liu parked the car in the lot where the office and lounge were located. After a moment's hesitation over whether to retrieve the umbrella he always kept in the trunk, he grabbed only the bouquet and stepped out of the car.

Perhaps because it was a weekday and the weather was poor, only three or four cars were parked there. The surroundings were so quiet that the sound of the drizzle settling on the trees was clearly audible. A man who appeared to be around Liu's age heard the car and opened the office door to look out. Standing on the threshold, he gruffly stated that the bouquet could not be left at the memorial hall. Liu assured him he'd take the flowers home with him. The man looked Liu up and down with a wary gaze before disappearing back into the office.

Liu began walking up the incline behind the parking lot. The path leading to the memorial hall building was well-paved, but visitors were not allowed to drive up.

The memorial hall building was situated in a cozy location, as if embraced by the forest. The area around the building was as eerily quiet as the parking lot below.

Liu smoothed the front of his coat for no particular reason, then climbed the polished granite steps and went inside.

He could hear the hushed voices of visitors. No faces were visible — only voices, and there seemed to be very few of them.

Liu walked slowly down the corridor between the large wooden display cases holding urns, searching for the section Yeehyeon had indicated. Although the wall near the entrance had windows to let in natural light, the interior felt dim even with the lights on. It was probably the weather.

Since the area was clearly sectioned off, much like a library, finding the niche was not difficult.

Liu's soft footsteps slowed, then stopped.

"......"

When Liu read the name engraved on the small memorial tablet beside the urn, the muscles on both sides of his jaw tightened.

Liu moistened his lower lip and respectfully clasped the bouquet in both hands in front of his waist. His head kept bowing as if a weight hung from his forehead. His emotions churned as though he were standing on the deck of a ship.

It took him aback. He hadn't expected to feel such agitation before the memorial tablet of someone he had never actually met, even if she was Yeehyeon's mother. He couldn't pinpoint the cause of the emotion welling up in him.

It probably wasn't direct mourning for the deceased, but rather sympathy, empathy, and guilt for everything Yeehyeon had to go through from her death.

Liu hadn't dared bring up Yeehyeon's mother with him, for fear of touching a wound that still bled. Yet he dreaded confessing the Changing to her even more than he had dreaded telling Manager Han or Suki Kim. That she was now ashes resting silently among countless other urns made no difference.

Liu pressed his lips together, trying to steady his expression, and took a ragged breath.

"Happy birthday."

The confession he wanted to make, the forgiveness he wanted to ask for, the promise he wanted to make for the future. Liu swallowed them all. The birthday greeting was all he could manage.

Manager Han had told him on the phone that the visit would probably be brief, since there was no grave to tend or food to lay out for bowing. But by the time Liu turned to leave — the Jana roses still in his hand, the same kind as when he'd proposed to Yeehyeon — over thirty minutes had already passed.

It was still drizzling when Liu stepped outside. Looking down at the misty forest just ahead, he let out a long breath. He desperately wanted a cigarette. He had refrained from smoking since waking up, feeling it wouldn't show proper respect.

He couldn't quite bring himself to leave. He stood under the concrete awning, his gaze fixed on the quiet, rainy landscape. Someone was approaching rapidly from the uphill path he had just climbed.

The man, wearing a backpack, walked with long, quick strides without an umbrella. His movement — the only dynamic thing in the surroundings — felt conspicuous.

When the man drew close enough for Liu to make out his features, Liu straightened from where he'd been leaning. A crease formed between his brows. The man climbing the steps, not even pausing under the awning to brush the rain from the shoulders of his jacket, was Yeehyeon's father.

A momentary crack appeared in the man's eyes. Liu knew at once the man had recognized him too. But the clash of gazes was fleeting. Yeehyeon's father walked right past Liu without even slowing his pace.

It took him about thirty minutes to come back out.

Liu had stayed for nearly an hour himself. Even so, with so little a visitor was allowed to do in there, he couldn't guess how the man was spending his time. He waited patiently.

It wasn't to say anything specific or hear anything in particular. But for some reason, Liu didn't want to pretend he hadn't seen him and walk away. It wasn't out of pity or courtesy. If he had to define it, it was closer to the opposite.

"......"

The man must have seen Liu from behind on his way out. Again he walked straight past and down the stairs. Liu had expected as much. He followed quietly, keeping about fifteen paces behind him.

At the fork where the parking lot and the entrance road diverged, Liu took the man by the upper arm and blocked his path. The man's eyes, which Liu now met directly and up close for the first time, were distinctly hazy, as if he had turned his back on the world. They were the kind of eyes that seemed to look straight through Liu at something behind him.

"Let me drive you. Please, get in."

"......"

The man lowered his gaze to the bouquet of Jana roses in Liu's hand. They stood facing each other in the drizzle against a backdrop of rain-darkened trees. At last the man walked over to the car and stood beside it.

Liu hadn't expected the man to accept. But there was no reason to be flustered. He placed the bouquet in the back seat and started the car. As if the silence were contagious, Liu kept his mouth firmly shut until they reached the village, about thirty minutes away in the opposite direction from the hotel. He had no desire to make small talk to win the man over or ease the tension.

As they drove along the coastal road with the East Sea to their left, the man hugged his backpack and stared out at the gray sea and sky.

It was only when the alleyway with the shark family mural — where Liu had once parked and waited to meet Yeehyeon — began to come into view that he slowed down and spoke.

"I went to the memorial hall... at Yeehyeonie's request."

The sea had long since vanished from view, but the man's gaze remained stubbornly fixed outside the window.

"Yeehyeonie is doing well there. He's healthy, fully dedicated to his work, and receiving recognition for it."

Yeehyeon kept in regular touch with his father's older brother and his wife. Liu deliberately gave the update in person so the man would know his son was still seeing Liu.

The man turned his head and looked down at the backpack on his lap. His eyelids blinked rapidly.

The two men fell silent again for a long time. Only the waves gave away the presence of the unseen sea. Liu said nothing to make him stay, yet the man didn't get out. And as long as he sat there, Liu didn't ask him to leave.

Liu tightened his grip on the top of the steering wheel. Being here with this man beside him was more painful than he'd expected. He let go of the lip he'd been biting, and the words came out almost in a sigh.

"He will probably... become an Omega before the year ends."

"......"

The man turned his gaze on Liu. Liu met his eyes squarely.

There was nothing unusual about the man's pupils or irises. Still, his eyes reminded Liu of his own when they turned white after the Changing. Now the man blinked, a sign of a response in that empty gaze.

So he does worry about his son after all?

A wicked impulse to mock him stirred. Liu's face twitched briefly with the effort of holding it back.

Liu knew Yeehyeon had told his father about the Changing. This time he felt none of the guilt that had made him shrink before the urn. Given what he'd done to Yeehyeon, he had no business judging anyone, yet anger boiled up in him at the man beside him. It felt much like self-hatred.

The thought of the wounds and shock this man had inflicted on Yeehyeon made Liu tremble with anger. Yet he couldn't be sure he'd have handled the same situation any better. Losing Yeehyeon. No, having him taken away. Yeehyeon simply disappearing one day. What in that hell could possibly give him the strength to live again?

Yeehyeon had forgiven Liu so much. Perhaps it was precisely because he and this man were alike that Liu couldn't bring himself to extend the same forgiveness to him.

Liu felt a strong urge to smoke again and looked away from the man first. As he unclenched his fist and ran a hand over his face, the man unzipped the backpack he had been holding so protectively.

Yeehyeon’s father pulled out a sketchbook about the size of a school notebook and waved it under Liu’s nose, urging him to take it. The man was capable of deep stillness, as if sunk into the abyssal depths, yet he would sometimes disrupt that balance with sudden, volatile movements.

Liu took the notebook, somewhat stunned.

"......"

Page after page of smooth drawing paper was packed with sketches and studies in pencil, oil-based colored pencil, and marker. Liu opened his mouth, but no sound came out.

After losing his wife, the man had turned his back on Yeehyeon and completely given up painting — as far as Liu knew. And when Yeehyeon left this village to go to Paris, he had left all his art supplies behind. Just in case. He hadn't known whether the bait he'd left would lead to disappointment or hope, and not knowing was still frightening — but it was better than the suffocating silence, so he had decided to throw something out there. Yeehyeon had told him this over cocktails during a date at a dim café in Paris.

The miscellaneous belongings and fishing gear piled up in a corner of the yard. The view of the sea from a cliff-like vantage point. The corroded gate. The scratched fish. The small dog scampering around.

The man had been showing his wife fragments of the narrow world he observed — those crude and pitiful cross-sections of daily life.

"......"

At the sound of the man opening the passenger door, Liu snapped his head up as if waking from a dream.

The man, having stepped out of the car, was looking at Liu with eyes where the boundary between the dark iris and the white of the eye was indistinct. They resembled the eyes of a mermaid forcibly dragged ashore in a net, or the eyes of a stag that had encountered a hunter's rifle while frantically digging through snow in midwinter, searching for food for its mate and young.

Liu bit his dry lower lip until it hurt. After standing there looking at him for a moment, the man closed the door, ascended the stairs leading to his house, and disappeared into the rain.

Liu curled the fingers of his left hand around the sketchbook cover and rubbed his ring as though reaching for a painkiller. He missed Yeehyeon so much he could hardly bear it.

· SEOUL ·

Manager Han repeatedly fiddled with the handle of the large mug before letting go. Her cup was about half full of coffee, but since she had barely touched it while listening to Liu's story, it must have gone cold by now.

"Shall I brew some more coffee?"

"......"

At Liu's question, she suddenly looked up. She met his gaze with a blank expression, as if she had heard an unexpected question, and slowly shook her head. Then, not wanting her thoughts interrupted, she lowered her gaze back to the mug. Liu ran a hand down his face from his forehead, then took a drink of beer and lit a cigarette.

Manager Han's silence continued for some time afterward. She puckered her lips a few times as if about to say something, but then clamped her mouth shut even tighter than before.

It was only after the freshly lit cigarette had burned down about halfway that she finally opened her mouth, seeming to have somewhat composed herself.

"Is there anyone else who knows besides me?"

"Shushu and Choi Inwu... Suki Kim."

Manager Han's eyes narrowed at the mention of the last name. Liu thought he knew why.

Although he had kept quiet about the Ghost, he had once told Manager Han about Suki Kim's efforts — how she had set aside her painting for a time and focused solely on that matter, in order to turn him into a Golden Alpha free from the domination of pheromones.

Knowing the kind of person Suki Kim was, Manager Han could fully anticipate — and was horrified by — how disappointed she must have been upon hearing Liu's confession.

Liu watched the smoke curling from the cigarette in his hand and added his explanation.

"Shushu thought we were undergoing the Changing by mutual agreement, and Choi Inwu... I asked him to go along with it to buy myself some time. That's when the topic came up."

"......"

"I visited my mother on the way back from Paris this time and told her myself."

"Then you never intended to tell Shushu or Inwu directly. Why are you telling me this?"

There was no accusation in her voice, no suggestion that she wished he'd spared her this ugly side of her friend and business partner. She simply wanted to know why he'd told her.

"Back then, I wasn't thinking about the future at all — I hadn't even planned to keep it secret forever. Now... I thought Manager Han had the right to know that I'm the one who committed this act."

Manager Han shook her head, the movement almost imperceptible. It seemed less like a denial of Liu and more like she still couldn't believe the situation. It was beyond her capacity to process. She seemed to be postponing any conclusion for the time being.

"And Ms. Kim... what did she say?"

Liu stubbed out the cigarette he had been holding without taking another drag since she spoke, and repeatedly opened and closed his empty fist in a meaningless gesture. He seemed to consider whether to answer her question directly, but even Liu's wry smile as he lowered his gaze was enough for her to anticipate the situation.

"Does Yeehyeonie know you told her?"

Liu bit down on his lower lip and nodded.

"Yeehyeon and I agreed we'd give my mother time and work through it together. For now... she's too disappointed...."

Manager Han let out a sigh and leaned back against the chair. For the first time since Liu began his story, she relaxed her shoulders and looked around with an unsteady gaze, as if seeing the place for the first time.

"I completely understand how Ms. Kim must feel. Honestly, right now, I don't even know how to process this. A Ghost, of all things... And Yeehyeonie too...."

Unable to finish her sentence, she roughly swept her hands over her face. She shook her head several times with force, trying to compose herself.

Manager Han's information about Ghosts wasn't much different from what Choi Inwu had. She had only vaguely heard the name before. To them, Ghosts were legendary beings, like vampires or yetis. They knew even less about Diamond Dust.

"If you plan on telling Yuni and Juhanie as well, I really hope you won't."

Unlike before, her tone was calm yet resolute, as if she had reached a firm conclusion on this specific point.

"I understand why you want to confess everything. But if you think about it from their perspective... that's not the right way to go. It's too much for them to bear. Just... stay as the Liu Weikun they've always known. And I'm sorry to say this, but you'll simply have to carry the guilt of keeping this secret yourself, Director Liu."

"......"

Liu was absently rubbing his ring when she asked him for a beer. He brought back two bottles, opened both, and handed her one. She drank about a third of it in one go, as though she were parched. Then she scratched at the bottle with her fingernail and lowered her voice as if she were about to tell him a secret.

"I know what it's like to be so deeply infatuated with someone that you can't see anything else. My partner was also a Beta."

When they first started working together at The Face, the two of them had quickly recognized they were cut from the same cloth — but because they were similar types, they never asked about or showed curiosity regarding each other's private lives.

Liu only found out that she had moved to Hong Kong with her artist husband after they had already decided to divorce and her husband had returned to Korea. Even when she told him that story, she hadn't gone into detail about the love story or the reasons for the divorce.

"The reason things didn't work out between us had absolutely nothing to do with the difference in our genders — Alpha and Beta — but... when I was completely obsessed, if I could have, I would have wanted to turn my partner into an Omega."

"Even though I hadn't been particularly conscious of being an Alpha, I nearly went crazy with anxiety because my partner wasn't an Omega — whether that's instinct or something else, I don't know. Because no matter how much attraction you feel on a human level, it's hard for Alphas and Omegas on the one hand, and Betas on the other, to understand what it's physically like to be the other person. Well... even Alphas and Omegas have that problem with each other."

"Loving someone. In the end, maybe it's just wanting the other person to remain beside you in the form you desire. At the same time, it's also the desire to remain in the form the other person desires, so you can keep being loved. Those two desires have to find a balance...."

Her eyes, tracing back over the past relationship in which she'd never found that balance, held neither regret nor longing. But one could sense a faint ache lingering around the scars.

In her, Liu saw a reflection of Shushu. He could now vaguely understand that feeling lingering pain from a past love didn't necessarily mean holding onto foolish attachment. Instead of condemning their feelings as stubborn, he could nod and share a drink with them — only after nearly losing his own love.

"Don't tell me that's why you avoided me and kept your distance from the Phantom work?"

"It wasn't that."

Liu shook his head and placed the shopping bag he had set beside him onto the dining table.

"What's this?"

"Oh, these are made from Yeehyeonie's work?"

A look of pleasure and pride spread across her face. Liu took another gulp of beer to gather his courage. It didn't help much.

Manager Han's face subtly stiffened as she placed a small notebook — printed with a collaborative work with Ben — onto the table.

"I want to properly support artists who have talent but struggle to find a gallery they can trust with their work. It will be similar in nature to The Hands, but I expect it will operate more like a sponsorship foundation. I plan to step away from Phantom entirely and focus solely on that. Of course, discussing the actual acquisition of Phantom is a conversation for if you want to take it over, Manager Han."

Manager Han, who had been listening to Liu with heavy eyes, shifted her gaze to the items spread out on the dining table. Most of the small-to-mid-sized galleries around Phantom that had taken on experimental projects recently were concurrently producing this kind of planned merchandise.

Contemporary art was no longer the exclusive domain of wealthy older individuals who stood back and maintained a dignified distance. The age range of those establishing small galleries or artist collectives was getting younger, as was the demographic that appreciated and consumed art. While Liu welcomed this change itself, he had always maintained a negative stance toward commercializing artwork and mass-producing it as merchandise.

Liu forced a smile and added.

He hadn't reached a conclusion about which method was more correct. It didn't mean the approach he had maintained until now was wrong, nor was it a surrender to the flow of the times.

Unlike Manager Han, Yuni, and recently Juhan, he had always maintained a certain distance, observing things from the sidelines. Whatever he did, it lacked intensity — it lacked desperation. So it was natural that everyone had found it strange when he rushed to open the New York branch as if he were being chased.

This was a habit he had previously dismissed as inconsequential. However, after Yeehyeon left and Liu began to scrutinize himself more strictly, he could no longer ignore that lukewarm quality. He had thought he was staking everything on Phantom, but he had never truly drawn everything out of himself.

He intended to hand over the operation to those who had poured more fervent affection into Phantom, and finally, truly, intensely, desperately, immerse himself in figuring out what he could throw himself into with complete commitment.

Manager Han trailed off, sighing. Despite her expectations, her expression was one of bewilderment.

"Take all the time you need to think it over. I can wait."

Even before Yeehyeon entered his life, there had been the lively warmth of people who cared for one another here. But even within that vibrant exchange, he had always held himself a few steps back. He had considered himself quite fortunate for the way he had lived: a trusted friend who was also his business partner, bright and diligent employees he wanted to support, a friend he'd grown up with like a brother, and even a friend with whom he could trade barbs.

But after Yeehyeon appeared in that landscape, everything changed.

The balance was broken, the gaps widened, and suddenly the emptiness in his life became starkly apparent. And into that emptiness, Yeehyeon's presence pushed in — warm and full.

The first person he had desired fiercely and desperately. The one whom he had wounded so deeply that the excuse of inexperience couldn't begin to cover it.
